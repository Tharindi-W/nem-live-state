Australia as a live testbed for AI that has to understand the world, not just describe it.

The opportunity is not another dashboard and not another chatbot. The deeper problem is whether an AI system can maintain a continuously updated belief about a messy, partially observed physical world, forecast several possible futures, and stay calibrated as reality changes.

01 | The problem

What are we actually trying to solve?

A crisp problem definition for a team discussion, without prematurely locking into a model architecture.

Can an AI system infer the hidden state of a live, high-dimensional environment from incomplete and asynchronous observations, then make calibrated forecasts about what happens next and how shocks propagate through the system?
This is different from asking an LLM to summarize a stream. A useful system has to preserve state across time, distinguish signal from noise, handle missing observations, understand spatial structure, model feedback loops, represent uncertainty, update its beliefs when evidence arrives, and be scored against what actually happened.

Why ordinary LLMs struggle

Text models are excellent at language and broad reasoning, but real systems are numerical, spatial, asynchronous and stateful. Compressing thousands of sensor observations into prose can throw away exactly the dynamics that matter.

Why this is scientifically interesting

The target is not just prediction accuracy. It is state estimation, probabilistic forecasting, causal response, counterfactual reasoning and adaptation under distribution shift.

Why this can become valuable

A small improvement in anticipating power prices, network disruption, fire spread, coastal risk, severe weather or asset stress can change real operational decisions with financial consequences.

02 | Australian live-data landscape

The strongest live and near-live systems

This is a practical shortlist of major Australian sources with enough continuity, structure and consequence to support serious forecasting research. "Free" and "commercially reusable" are deliberately treated as separate questions.

AEMO National Electricity Market

The NEM is one of the best naturally occurring AI benchmarks in Australia. Public dispatch data updates every 5 minutes and includes regional demand, prices, interconnector flows, constraints, generation and ancillary services. Five-minute pre-dispatch also publishes short-term price and demand forecasts.

Cadence5 minutes
AI difficultyVery high
Commercial valueExceptional

Hard problem: predict price spikes, congestion, renewable ramps, reserve stress and cascading effects from weather and outages. The graph structure changes and market participants react strategically.

Access caveat: AEMO makes extensive public data available, but multiple public pages state that specific datasets are provided for information and are not intended for commercial use. This is excellent research data, but a commercial product needs explicit legal and licensing review.

Best research wedge: electricity

The NEM gives fast labels, hard dynamics and obvious economic value. Forecasts can be scored every five minutes. The most compelling research question is whether a persistent state model can beat strong tabular/time-series baselines and a Pythia-style text-plus-LLM benchmark, especially during rare price or constraint regimes.

Important: use AEMO public feeds for research only until data reuse terms are reviewed. A commercial implementation may need a separate licensed data path or customer-provided market data.

Cleanest open-data wedge: ocean, hazards, air and transport

IMOS, DEA Hotspots, NSW air quality and several transport feeds have clearer Creative Commons reuse paths. They are ideal for an early prototype whose data rights are easier to explain to partners.

Advantage: these domains also naturally combine. For example, weather plus fire hotspots plus air quality, or ocean plus weather plus coastal assets.
04 | Why this is more than forecasting

The real benchmark is state, uncertainty and consequence

Predicting the next number is useful, but it is not yet evidence of a world model.

State estimation

Can the system infer important hidden variables that are not directly observed, and keep those beliefs consistent as new sensors arrive at different times?

Multi-step forecasting

Does it remain stable when rolled forward, or does error explode? Can it forecast trajectories rather than one-step point estimates?

Uncertainty calibration

If it says 70 percent, do events actually occur about 70 percent of the time? GenCast's success is a useful reminder that uncertainty is not a side feature in chaotic systems.

Cross-system effects

Can a weather shock alter the model's beliefs about electricity, transport, smoke or coastal risk in the right direction and at the right timescale?

Counterfactuals

What happens if an interconnector fails, a train line closes, wind production falls, a fire jumps a containment line, or a storm track shifts? A useful system should reason about interventions, not only extrapolate.

Distribution shift

The important moments are often the rare ones. The system has to notice when the current regime is unlike its training history and widen uncertainty instead of becoming confidently wrong.

A strong result is not "our model predicts well on average." A strong result is "our model remains calibrated when the system enters a regime that matters operationally."
05 | GitHub precedents worth studying

Pythia, MiroFish and Osiris are directly relevant

They should be in the conversation because they demonstrate strong developer interest in live world state, forecasting and simulation. They also help expose the gap between live-data fusion and a learned dynamical world model.

Pythia by jangles-byte

Pythia combines live global feeds, a local LLM forecasting layer, a forecast ledger, what-if analysis and a specialist council. It is MIT licensed and explicitly positions itself as a way to give agents a live view of world events plus forecasts.

Why it matters here: Pythia is an excellent baseline for the thesis. Its current pipeline groups events into a compact prose world brief, sends that brief to an LLM for predictions, and then has LLM personas re-score forecasts. That is powerful, but it is still primarily text-mediated reasoning over a compressed snapshot.

Repository · world_state.py · oracle.py · swarm.py

MIT

MiroFish by 666ghj

MiroFish presents itself as a swarm-intelligence prediction engine that constructs a parallel digital world from seed information, generates agents with memory and behaviour, and runs social simulation before generating reports.

Why it matters here: it takes simulation and emergence seriously. It is conceptually closer to "rehearse possible futures" than a standard forecasting dashboard. It is especially relevant for human and institutional systems where agent interactions matter.

Commercial note: the repository is AGPL-3.0. Study the ideas freely, but direct incorporation into a proprietary product needs careful licence review.

Repository · Project site

AGPL-3.0

Osiris by simplifaisoul

Osiris is an open-source real-time global intelligence platform and is one of the feed-layer projects Pythia builds on.

Why it matters here: it is evidence that normalising many live sources into a single world-state surface has stand-alone value. For this research direction, the more interesting next question is what learns the dynamics after the live state has been assembled.

Repository

MIT

Aurora by Microsoft Research

Aurora is a concrete proof that a model trained on heterogeneous Earth-system data can learn transferable dynamics. The Nature paper reports a foundation model trained on more than one million hours of geophysical data and strong results across weather, air quality, waves and tropical cyclone tracks.

Why it matters here: it shows the core thesis is not science fiction. A shared latent representation can transfer across related physical tasks. The unanswered opportunity is how far this idea can extend into cyber-physical systems such as grids, mobility and infrastructure.

Microsoft Research paper page · GitHub

Research precedent

What these projects collectively suggest

Pythia and Osiris: live world-state aggregation is practical. MiroFish: simulation and multi-agent emergence are compelling for social systems. Aurora, GraphCast and GenCast: learned dynamics can beat or complement expensive physical forecasting pipelines in structured Earth systems.

The open question worth attacking is therefore not "can we connect more feeds?" It is "can we learn a persistent, calibrated representation of a live operational system that predicts consequences better than strong domain baselines and text-centric agent systems?"

06 | Why Australia is unusually useful

A country-sized laboratory with public instrumentation

The combination matters more than any one dataset.

Fast feedback

Power dispatch, transport movement, road incidents, radar and fire observations update quickly enough to run continuous forecast experiments and get ground truth the same day.

Natural extremes

Bushfires, severe storms, heat, floods, coastal hazards and renewable ramps create exactly the regime changes that expose weak models.

Strong open-data culture

DEA, IMOS, transport agencies and environmental agencies publish substantial machine-readable data, often with standard formats or Creative Commons licences.

Multiple jurisdictions

NSW and Victoria give comparable but non-identical transport and environmental systems. NEM and WEM give different electricity regimes. Transfer can be tested inside one country.

Global problem shapes

Electricity grids, GTFS-R mobility, weather, ocean observations, wildfire monitoring and air pollution all have international equivalents. The research can transfer beyond Australia.

Commercial consequence

Energy, logistics, insurance, infrastructure, emergency management, mining, agriculture, coastal operations and transport all pay for better anticipation of system state and risk.

07 | How to start without overbuilding

Start with a falsifiable benchmark, not a grand platform

The fastest way to learn whether there is something real here is to create an evaluation loop where failure is obvious.

Write one problem sentence.Example: "Given the live state of the NEM at time t, forecast the probability distribution of regional price, demand, constraint activation and interconnector flow over the next 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes." Keep the initial target measurable.
Create the source and licence registry before the model.For every feed record URL, owner, update cadence, schema, history, access method, rate limit, licence, attribution requirement and whether commercial use is explicit, restricted or unclear.
Archive the live stream from day one.Public sites frequently keep short rolling windows. A research system needs its own timestamped archive so that every forecast can be reproduced and scored later.
Build brutally strong baselines.Persistence, seasonal averages, gradient-boosted trees, temporal convolution, graph/time-series models, official forecasts where available, and a Pythia-style LLM baseline. A new idea matters only if it beats something credible.
Score probability, not just point error.Use Brier score, log loss, calibration curves, interval coverage and event-specific metrics. Separately score common periods and rare high-impact regimes.
Test transfer.If the first environment is NSW transport, evaluate zero-shot or light fine-tuning on Victoria. If the first environment is energy, test whether representations transfer between regions or between NEM and WEM tasks.
Add one interacting system only after the first benchmark works.Examples: weather into electricity, road incidents into public transport, weather and hotspots into air quality, weather into ocean waves. This is where cross-system state becomes scientifically interesting.
Only then ask what customers will pay for.The product should emerge from a measurable forecasting advantage or a measurable reduction in decision latency, not from a generic "AI for live data" pitch.
First milestone: a continuously running leaderboard where every model makes timestamped probabilistic forecasts and cannot rewrite history after reality arrives.
Pythia's public forecast-ledger idea is directionally excellent. Borrow the discipline: every prediction gets a timestamp, horizon, probability and eventual resolution. The research becomes much more credible when the scorecard is automatic and unforgiving.
08 | Commercial lens

Where the economic value can become very large

These are problem categories, not product commitments.

Energy operations and trading

Price and congestion forecasting, renewable uncertainty, storage dispatch, outage risk, reserve stress and asset scheduling. Small improvements can affect high-value decisions frequently.

Insurance and catastrophe risk

Live updates to hazard probability and exposure as weather, fire, flood, waves or seismic events evolve. The valuable output is a changing distribution of loss, not a static alert.

Transport and logistics

Predict network recovery, delay propagation, route reliability and downstream capacity impacts rather than merely reporting where a disruption already exists.

Mining and remote infrastructure

Weather, roads, power, water and logistics interact. A system that understands cross-domain operational risk could be more valuable than separate dashboards.

Coastal and maritime operations

Wave, wind, current and temperature forecasts affect ports, offshore construction, aquaculture, vessels and coastal infrastructure.

Government and emergency management

Resource positioning, scenario planning and cascading-impact forecasts across fire, flood, roads, health and infrastructure. This demands careful safety boundaries and human oversight.

10 | Data rights and operational reality

Publicly visible does not automatically mean product-ready

This is one of the most important findings from the source review.

Strong reuse candidates

  • IMOS data: generally CC BY 4.0, with attribution requirements.
  • DEA Hotspots: CC BY 4.0 and multiple machine-readable access methods.
  • NSW air quality: Creative Commons Attribution in Data.NSW.
  • Transport Victoria GTFS-R and unplanned disruptions: CC BY 4.0, with API-key access.
  • Several TfNSW realtime datasets: Creative Commons Attribution, with free account and API key.

Research first, legal review before product

  • BOM anonymous feeds: free, but explicitly not for commercial use.
  • AEMO pages for dispatch, demand and pre-dispatch: public, but specific pages state data is not intended for commercial use.
  • Transport Victoria Bluetooth travel time: CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • Any source whose public website is easy to scrape but does not publish a reuse licence should be treated as unclear, not assumed open.
Commercial rule: the prototype data stack and the eventual production data stack do not have to be identical. The research question can be validated using public non-commercial feeds, then production can use licensed feeds, customer-owned telemetry or negotiated access. What matters now is being explicit about the boundary.
11 | Research evidence

There is already strong evidence that learned dynamics can matter

The relevant lesson from modern forecasting models is not that one architecture solves every domain. It is that structured models of state evolution can outperform expensive or purely descriptive approaches.

World Models

Ha and Schmidhuber's 2018 work formalised the idea of learning a compressed spatial and temporal representation of an environment and using the learned model for downstream control.

World Models paper

GraphCast

DeepMind demonstrated that graph-based learned dynamics can produce highly skilful global medium-range weather forecasts quickly.

GraphCast overview

GenCast

GenCast shifted the emphasis toward probabilistic ensembles, with the Nature paper reporting stronger skill than ECMWF ENS across most evaluated targets and better handling of extreme-weather risk.

GenCast in Nature

Aurora

Aurora shows that a shared Earth-system representation can be fine-tuned across air quality, ocean waves, tropical cyclone tracks and weather. This is particularly relevant to the idea of learning reusable dynamics rather than one isolated predictor.

Aurora in Nature
